01 · Fundamentals — Chapter 08

The world is continuous. Computers are not.

Every PLC, every controller, every piece of digital electronics has to bridge that gap. This chapter is about exactly how a continuously-varying physical quantity becomes a discrete number — and how that number becomes physical action again.

PATH: ANALOG
real world → sensor → analog / digital representation → controller live signal chain
01 — 05 · First Principles

Continuous correspondence vs. discrete states

Analog means one physical quantity is represented by another quantity that varies continuously in correspondence with it — temperature rising smoothly, voltage rising smoothly right along with it, no gaps.

Digital divides that same information into discrete states or numerical values. Watch the two waveforms: the top trace never stops moving; the bottom one only exists at the marked instants.

Analog ≠ voltage. Digital ≠ computer. These words describe how information is represented, not which physical medium carries it.

09 · The Failure Mode

Aliasing, isolated

f_s > 2 × f_max — the basic requirement for capturing a signal faithfully. Violate it, and a genuinely fast signal produces sample points that are indistinguishable from a much slower one. The digital system has no way to know the difference; both signals produce the exact same samples.

An anti-aliasing filter — a low-pass filter ahead of the ADC — removes frequency content the sampling rate can't represent, before it ever reaches the sampler.

15 · The Practical Payoff

Noise — analog vs. digital

Watch the top trace: noise directly and permanently alters the analog value — there's no way to separate signal from corruption after the fact.

Watch the bottom trace: a digital receiver only has to decide which side of a threshold a noisy pulse landed on. Small noise gets regenerated back to a clean 0 or 1 — not immunity, but a real, threshold-based advantage.

PWM — digital switching, analog effect

The big mental model

      ↓

      ↓

Analog describes continuously varying representation. Digital describes discrete representation. Modern automation combines both because the physical world is continuous while controllers are fundamentally digital.
